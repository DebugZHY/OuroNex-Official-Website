OuroNex starts from Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area. That is not only where the team is based. It is the practice environment the work is designed around, and it shapes what we build and what we choose not to claim.

Structural work here is often tall, tight and heavily serviced. Load paths are shared across storeys, changes arrive while the model is live, and review has to survive a fast programme. A workflow designed for that setting must be conservative about assumptions, because so much depends on the handoff between analysis, drawings and site.

## Bilingual sets and submission culture

Bilingual drawings and statutory submission are part of the daily work, not an edge case. Notes, schedules and specifications move between languages, and the package must satisfy a submission process with its own rules about what is shown and how it is signed. Assistance that cannot follow a bilingual set, or cannot point to the clause or note it used, is of limited value here.

> Local practice is not a market detail. It is part of the specification: tools have to fit the way work is submitted, reviewed and signed in this region.

For our own direction, this affects both layers. SIDA, the platform concept, is an orchestration layer over engineering context — specialist agents working with project knowledge, codes and tools, with professional review closing the loop. SODA, the numerical workflow, is built on established numerical methods with explicit assumptions and engineering review. Neither is presented as finished, and local engagement is how requirements become concrete.

Working regionally also keeps the feedback loop short. Practitioners here can tell us where a workflow fails long before a general product would notice. Related engineering fields follow as the practice develops, but the starting point stays local — and the region remains the reference case while the work matures.
