SIDA — Super-Intelligent Design & Analysis — is the direction we are building towards, not a product you can license today. That distinction matters to us, and it shapes how we talk about it. This note is an attempt to say plainly what the concept is, what is still under research, and what we will not claim until we can show it.

The concept is orchestration. Structural engineering work already involves many specialised kinds of reasoning — reading a drawing set, checking a load path, reviewing a calculation, comparing a design against a code clause. Today these live in separate tools and separate heads. SIDA is the idea that specialist agents, each good at one of these tasks, can be coordinated over a shared engineering context, so the reasoning moves with the work instead of being reconstructed at every handover.

## What exists, and what does not

The pieces that exist are modest and specific. We can prepare engineering context so that an assistant can work over it. We can structure a review so that sources, assumptions and working travel with a result. We can run scoped pilots where a single workflow is instrumented end to end. These are workflow capabilities, and they are useful on their own.

The pieces that do not exist are equally specific. We do not have a general orchestration layer that composes specialist agents reliably across arbitrary projects. We do not claim autonomous design, and we do not claim that any part of the professional judgement can be delegated to software. Responsibility stays with the engineer, and any system we ship is designed around that constraint rather than around its absence.

> The honest description of SIDA today is a research direction with working pieces, not a platform with a roadmap of features. We would rather say that plainly than borrow credibility from a concept.

What comes next is deliberately unglamorous: more scoped engagements, more review gates exercised on real work, and a public record of what the approach can and cannot do. When the evidence supports a stronger claim, we will make it. Until then, the concept stays labelled as a concept — and the working pieces stay available for review on their own merits.
