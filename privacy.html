Legal/Privacy

Privacy Policy

OuroNex Technology Company LimitedLast updated 2 November 2026

OuroNex Technology Company Limited ("OuroNex", "we", "us" or "our") builds software and services for structural engineering practice. This policy explains what personal data we collect through our website and products, why, how we handle it, and the rights you have.

SIDA tools run locally on your own device, so your project information and engineering data are not uploaded to our servers by default; the personal data we do receive is limited to what we need to run the site, provide access and answer you, and where the detail depends on the service or the law we say so rather than implying more certainty than we have.

1. Scope and who we are

1.1 Who we are

OuroNex Technology Company Limited is based in Hong Kong and works with practices across Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area; our website is ouronex.com, and we are the data user responsible for personal data collected through it under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance of Hong Kong (the "PDPO").

1.2 What this policy covers

This policy applies to personal data we handle in connection with:

  • the OuroNex website, including pages, forms, downloads and any account area;
  • SIDA (Super-Intelligent Design & Analysis), our AI-assisted software direction for structural engineering workflows, and related desktop tools;
  • pilots, evaluation access, early-access programmes and demonstration environments;
  • accounts, licences, subscriptions and entitlements;
  • professional services, including workflow setup, training and support.

1.3 What this policy does not cover

It does not cover data processed entirely on your device or in systems you control, third-party websites and services (Section 15), or recruitment and employment data.

1.4 Our roles

For website, account, billing, support and marketing data we are the data user; where a customer asks us to process personal data belonging to its projects, staff or clients, the customer is the data user and we act as its service provider, or data processor (Section 6).

1.5 Other documents, and terms used here

Read this policy with our Terms of Service and any signed order form or pilot agreement, which prevails for the subject it covers. Personal data means information about an identified or identifiable individual as used in the PDPO; project data means the models, drawings and reports you work with in our tools; and local processing means processing on your device without sending content to us.

2. Summary of our approach

2.1 Local-first by design

Structural work is often confidential before it is anything else, so we design on the basis that engineering content stays where the work happens: on your machine, under your control. You can read the public parts of our website without an account.

2.2 Minimal collection

Where we ask for information we ask for what the task needs: an address to reply to, an account identifier, a billing contact, the details of a support question.

2.3 What we do not do

  • We do not sell, rent or trade personal data.
  • We do not upload your project files in the background.
  • We do not use project content to train general models unless a written agreement says otherwise.
  • We do not run third-party advertising networks on our website.
  • We do not make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you by machine alone.

2.4 Your control, and our limits

Section 12 explains how to ask what we hold, correct it, object, withdraw consent and complain, and Section 13 covers marketing. No system is perfectly secure, so Section 11 describes what we do rather than promising that nothing will go wrong.

3. Personal data we collect

The categories below describe what we may collect; not every category applies to every person, because a visitor who reads the website generates technical records and little else.

3.1 Account, contact and profile data

Name, work email address, organisation, role, region or language preference, telephone number if you give one, and the identifiers needed to authenticate you, with passwords hashed. We record account status, entitlements, licence identifiers and key account events, and keep the messages you send us so that we can answer them.

3.2 Billing, subscription and transaction data

Billing contact and address, plan or engagement details, invoice and tax information where the law requires it, transaction references and payment status. Card and bank details are entered with the payment provider, and we do not store full card numbers.

3.3 Support, feedback and complaint data

Correspondence and whatever you attach: screenshots, log extracts, sample files and the steps that led to the issue. Support records can therefore contain personal data inside the material you send, so please send only what is needed.

3.4 Website technical and usage data, and cookies

Technical records generated when pages are requested: IP address, browser type and version, device and operating system, page requested and referring page, time, language, and error or performance events. We derive only a coarse location from an IP address, and cookies are used as described in Section 7.

3.5 Early access, pilots and business communications

Applications ask for contact details, organisation, team size, discipline, region, the workflows you want to test, and eligibility information; pilots may add feedback, workshop notes, agreed usage measurements and reported outcomes. We also keep business contact details, event registrations and how you opted in or out.

4. Data we do not collect by default

4.1 Project content stays local

By default we do not collect, receive or store your project models, drawings, analysis files, calculation sheets, schedules, reports or the documents you open in our tools; those files live where you choose, and we keep no copy.

4.2 What local processing means in practice

When you run a SIDA tool the work happens on your machine using your own files, and results are written where you direct them, so a project's confidentiality does not depend on a transfer to us. Our tools read the files needed for the operation you request.

4.3 The limited traffic that does occur

Licensed software still involves some network activity: our tools may check a licence or entitlement, check for updates, download documentation or fetch product notices, and those requests identify an account, installation or version rather than project content. Diagnostics, where offered, are described and can be declined.

4.4 When content does leave your device

We will not claim that data never leaves your device, because it does when you decide it should: you send a file or log extract to support; you use an online feature that cannot work without transmitting the input; you join a pilot with agreed sharing; or you export material to another service. In each case we process what you send for the purpose you sent it.

5. How we use personal data and lawful bases

5.1 The purposes

We use personal data for these purposes and for purposes directly related to them:

  1. Providing access: accounts, sign-in verification, licences, entitlements, software, updates and documentation.
  2. Performing our agreements: quotes, pilot and services delivery, renewals, invoices, payments and disputes.
  3. Support: answering questions, diagnosing problems and following up.
  4. Security and integrity: preventing fraud, abuse and unauthorised access, and enforcing our Terms of Service.
  5. Improving what we build: understanding feature use and aggregate feedback, without project content unless a written agreement permits it.
  6. Service communication: changes, security matters, maintenance, and the status of your account.
  7. Marketing: product news, pilot invitations, events and practice notes, subject to Section 13.
  8. Legal obligations: records required by tax, accounting and corporate law, lawful requests, and legal claims.
  9. Corporate transactions: financing, merger, acquisition or transfer of assets, subject to Section 8.5.

5.2 The basis we rely on

The PDPO does not use the "lawful bases" list found in some other privacy laws; it requires a lawful purpose directly related to the data user's functions, adequate but not excessive data, use consistent with the purpose of collection, and consent for direct marketing. Within that framework we rely on:

  • Consent for marketing, optional features, pilots involving shared material, and uses described at collection as needing agreement, which you may withdraw (Section 12.5);
  • necessity for a contract or requested service, to open an account, provide access, deliver an engagement or take payment;
  • legitimate interests balanced against your rights, for security, service integrity, fraud prevention, support records, aggregate analysis and administration, which you may object to (Section 12.4);
  • legal obligation, to keep or disclose information as required by law, a court order or a regulator;
  • vital interests and legal claims, to protect life or safety, or to establish, exercise or defend a claim.

5.3 Aggregated information and new purposes

We may create statistics by combining records and removing details that could identify a person, and we do not attempt to re-identify that data. If we want to use personal data for a materially different purpose we will explain it and, where the law requires, ask first.

6. AI-assisted features and project content

6.1 What "AI-assisted" means here

SIDA helps with drafting, checking, referencing codes and documentation, and organising a workflow. It is assistive software: it produces suggestions, not certified engineering judgements, and it is expected to be used by qualified practitioners who review what it produces.

6.2 Local processing and online features

By default, project content is processed locally as described in Section 4 and is not uploaded to us; where a feature needs to reach a service — a model that cannot run on your machine, an online reference, or collaboration — the point of use says what would be sent, and you decide.

6.3 Who is responsible for project data

Project data frequently contains personal data: names and contact details of project staff, clients and site personnel, signatures on submissions, and photographs of sites and of people on them. Responsibility follows the work:

  • The customer is responsible for its project data, deciding what to collect, why, who may see it, how long to keep it, and how to handle requests from the individuals concerned; the customer is the data user.
  • OuroNex acts as a service provider where it processes personal data on a customer's behalf, for example when a customer asks us to look at a file during support; we then act only on documented instructions, keep the data confidential, protect it as described in Section 11, and return or delete it when the engagement ends, subject to legal retention.

6.4 Other people's personal data

If you submit material containing another person's personal data — a colleague's name, a client's contact details, a site photograph — you need the authority to do so, normally from your role and your organisation's notices. Do not submit special categories or unnecessary identifying details; redact what is not needed.

6.5 Inputs, outputs and quality records

Where an online AI-assisted feature is used, the input you provide and the output generated are processed to provide it, and we keep such records only as long as the feature needs them unless a longer period is disclosed. Outputs may be logged for quality, safety or abuse prevention only where that is disclosed and permitted, and we do not use customer content to train general models unless the agreement permits it.

6.6 Review before you rely on an output

AI-assisted output can be incomplete, out of date or simply wrong: it may misread a drawing, apply a clause incorrectly, or miss a constraint an experienced engineer would see. It is a starting point for professional review, and responsibility for judgement, checking and sign-off stays with the qualified people doing the work.

6.7 Deployments run by your organisation

Where SIDA is deployed by your employer or another organisation, that organisation usually controls the account and the associated data, so ask it first about privacy requests; it is the data user, and we support it in responding.

7. Cookies and similar technologies

7.1 What they are, and the categories we use

Cookies are small text files a site stores in your browser; similar technologies include local and session storage, pixels, and identifiers used by software development kits. The categories that may apply are:

  • Strictly necessary: session handling, form protection, load balancing, abuse prevention, and remembering your cookie choices.
  • Preferences: remembering settings such as language or layout. The public site currently stores your language preference in your browser.
  • Measurement and advertising: we do not currently run third-party analytics, advertising networks or cross-site trackers on our website; our servers keep ordinary request records for security, diagnostics and basic operational statistics. If we introduce a non-essential measurement or advertising technology, we will update this policy and, where required, ask for your consent before it is set.

7.2 Similar technologies in our software

Our desktop tools may store preferences, licensing state and interface settings locally so the software behaves consistently between sessions; that storage is part of the product working locally, not a mechanism for sending project content to us.

7.3 Managing cookies, signals and embedded content

You can control cookies in your browser — accept, block third-party cookies, be notified, or delete them — and change your choices in any consent tool we present; blocking strictly necessary cookies may break sign-in and forms, while blocking measurement cookies does not affect access. We honour legally required opt-out signals, and an embedded third-party video or map may set its own cookies under its own policy.

8. Sharing and disclosure

8.1 We do not sell personal data

We do not sell, rent, trade or otherwise provide personal data to third parties for their own marketing, and we do not do so in exchange for payment or other benefit.

8.2 Service providers, and the controls on them

We use other organisations — hosting and infrastructure, IT and security support, communications and email, payment and billing, support tooling, and measurement where deployed — and they receive only the data needed for their task. Our written terms require them to act on our instructions, keep data confidential, apply appropriate security, restrict access, report incidents, and return or delete data when the engagement ends.

8.3 Affiliates and professional advisers

Where we work with affiliated companies we may share personal data for the purposes in Section 5, under this policy and equivalent obligations, and we may share data with lawyers, accountants, auditors, insurers and other advisers who need it to advise us, subject to professional confidentiality.

8.4 Authorities and legal process

We may disclose personal data where required or permitted by law: in response to a court order, a statutory demand, or a lawful request from a regulator or law-enforcement body. We check each request's validity and scope, provide only what it requires, and tell you unless prohibited. We may also disclose data where we reasonably believe it necessary to protect the rights, safety or property of OuroNex, our customers or the public, or to investigate abuse or respond to a security incident.

8.5 Corporate transactions

In a financing, merger, acquisition, restructuring, sale of assets or similar transaction personal data may be shared with the other parties and their advisers, or transferred with the transaction; we limit what is shared, require confidentiality, and ensure a recipient is bound by this policy or comparable terms.

8.6 With your consent, and sharing you control

We may share data where you ask us to — an integrated tool you enable, a named partner, a reference you agree to — and some sharing is entirely yours, such as exporting a report. We do not control data once you send it from your own device, and we may publish aggregate statistics that contain no personal data.

9. International transfers and safeguards

9.1 Where your data goes, and the safeguards we apply

We are based in Hong Kong and hold business records primarily there, but some service providers, advisers or group companies may operate elsewhere. Where data is transferred we apply:

  • contractual terms requiring confidentiality, security, purpose limitation and appropriate handling;
  • transfers limited to the data a recipient needs, with access restricted to named roles;
  • encryption in transit, and protection of records at rest by the recipient;
  • assessment of the legal and security environment of the destination, including the risk of access by public authorities;
  • review when the recipient, the location or the nature of the data changes.

9.2 Hong Kong law and transfers

Hong Kong law does not currently impose a general statutory restriction on transferring personal data outside Hong Kong, and we do not pretend otherwise; we treat transfer as a risk to be managed, and the safeguards above apply whether or not a statute compels them.

9.3 Other legal systems

This policy is written primarily against Hong Kong law and the PDPO; if a mandatory law in another jurisdiction gives you a right that goes further, we will honour it to the extent it applies. If you want to know whether your data is transferred and what safeguards apply, ask us using the channel in Section 17.

10. Retention

10.1 Our principle

We keep personal data only as long as we need it for the purpose we collected it, and for as long afterwards as the law, a contract or the defence of a legal claim requires; after that we delete it or remove the details that identify individuals.

10.2 What that means by category

  • Account and profile data: while the account is active and briefly afterwards for reactivation, disputes and record-keeping.
  • Billing and transaction records: for the period required by tax, accounting and corporate record-keeping law, typically longer than the life of an account.
  • Support and complaint records: while the issue is open and for a reasonable period afterwards.
  • Website technical logs: for short periods for security and diagnostics, then deleted or aggregated.
  • Marketing records: while you are on our list, and afterwards a minimal opt-out record.
  • Pilot and services records: for the period in the relevant agreement, and no longer than the engagement and its reasonable aftermath require.

10.3 Legal retention and backups

We do not publish a single retention period, because records are kept for different reasons and one number would either mislead you or force us to delete records we must keep. Where the law requires us to keep a record we will not delete it on request, and we will say which records are affected. Backups are kept briefly and used only for recovery.

11. Security

11.1 Technical and organisational measures

We protect personal data with measures that match its sensitivity: encryption in transit, least-privilege access, authentication controls for administrative access, logging and monitoring, prompt security updates, separated environments, and backups tested by recovery. We keep written information-security and incident-response policies, require confidentiality from the people who work with us, and require service providers to meet comparable standards (Section 8.2).

11.2 Local-first reduces exposure, but nothing is absolute

Because project content stays on your device by default, there is no central store of engineering data for us to lose, which materially reduces risk without removing it, since the records we do hold still require protection. No organisation can guarantee absolute security, and we do not claim to.

11.3 If something goes wrong

If we become aware of a security incident affecting personal data we will investigate, contain it and take remedial action, and where the law requires notification we will notify affected individuals and the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data, Hong Kong without undue delay, telling you what we know and what you can do.

11.4 Your part

Keep your credentials confidential, use a unique password, enable multi-factor authentication where we offer it, keep your operating system and our software up to date, use device encryption where available, and tell us promptly if you suspect your account has been compromised.

12. Your rights

12.1 Your rights under the PDPO

If we hold personal data about you, you have the right to request access to it and to request correction of anything inaccurate, and you also have the right to object to particular uses, to withdraw consent where we rely on it, to ask us to delete data where that applies, and to complain to the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data, Hong Kong.

12.2 Access and correction

You can ask us to confirm whether we hold personal data about you and to provide a copy, and we will ask you to verify your identity and for details that help us locate the records. We may charge an access fee where the law permits and will tell you the amount beforehand; correction is free, and where we have shared data that is corrected we will tell the recipients if practicable.

12.3 Deletion and account closure

You can ask us to delete personal data where we no longer need it, where you have withdrawn consent, or where the law requires it, but deletion is not absolute: records covered by a legal retention requirement, or needed for a legal claim, must be kept (Section 10.3). Closing an account ends your access; it does not by itself erase records we must keep.

12.4 Objection and restriction

You can object to processing we base on our legitimate interests, and we will stop unless we have compelling grounds that outweigh yours or the processing is needed for a legal claim; objection to marketing always takes effect, without any balancing test (Section 13).

12.5 Portability and withdrawal of consent

Where the law that applies to you provides a right to receive your data in a structured, machine-readable format, we will provide it; project data is not held by us by default, so there is nothing for us to export. Where we rely on consent you can withdraw it at any time, and withdrawal stops that processing without affecting what we lawfully did before.

12.6 Complaints to the Privacy Commissioner

If you are not satisfied with how we have handled your data or your request, please tell us first, because most issues can be resolved quickly. You also have the right to complain to the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data, Hong Kong, whose website at pcpd.org.hk explains how, and we will cooperate with the Commissioner's office.

12.7 Timing and our limits

We aim to acknowledge requests promptly and to respond within a reasonable period after verifying your identity, and we will tell you if a request needs longer. We may decline where the law allows — where it would reveal another person's data without consent, where privilege applies, where it is repeated or vexatious, or where it would require disproportionate effort — and will explain why.

13. Marketing choices

13.1 What we send, and on what basis

We may send product updates, pilot and early-access invitations, event notices, and occasional notes on engineering practice. Where the PDPO requires consent for direct marketing we ask for it and record when you gave it; we do not provide your data to third parties for their own marketing, and we do not do so for gain.

13.2 How to opt out, and what you cannot opt out of

Every marketing message includes a way to unsubscribe, and you can also opt out through the channel in Section 17 without giving a reason; opting out is free and we keep only a minimal record of your choice. Messages about your account, security, an engagement, or a legal or policy change are part of the service and cannot be unsubscribed while you hold an account. If we telephone you for marketing we will comply with any do-not-call rules.

14. Children and vulnerable persons

14.1 Who our services are for

Our website and products are built for professional engineering practice and the organisations that employ or engage practitioners; they are not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children.

14.2 Age and authority

You should be at least 18 years old, or have the involvement of a parent or guardian and the authority of the organisation that holds the account; if you are under 18, please do not create an account or send us personal data without that involvement.

14.3 If we learn we have collected a child's data

If we become aware that we have collected a child's personal data without appropriate authority, we will take reasonable steps to delete it, subject to any legal requirement to keep it, and we may close the associated account; if you believe we hold such data, contact us through the channel in Section 17.

14.4 Children in project data

Project material can include images or details of minors, for example in a site photograph; where a customer's project data contains such information the customer is responsible for it (Section 6.3), including having a lawful basis and handling any request from a guardian.

14.5 Vulnerable persons

Where someone cannot manage their own privacy choices, a person with lawful authority — a guardian, or an attorney acting under a valid instrument — can make a request on their behalf; we will ask for evidence of that authority and handle the request with the same care as any other.

15. Third-party links and services

15.1 Links from our website

Our website may link to third-party sites, articles or partner pages; those sites are not covered by this policy, because the organisation behind them handles your data under its own privacy notice, which may differ from ours.

15.2 Services you use alongside ours

Application stores, distribution platforms, payment pages, sign-in providers, email services and event platforms are operated by other organisations and collect data under their own policies; we receive only what we need for our part, such as an account identifier and email address from a sign-in provider, or confirmation of a transaction from a payment page.

15.3 Integrations you configure

Our tools may be configured to work with storage, document systems or other services you or your organisation choose; those integrations are between you and the provider, so you control the connection, the credentials and the data exchanged, that provider's terms apply, and we do not control its retention.

15.4 No endorsement, and data that reaches us

Linking to a third party is not an endorsement of its privacy practices, and we cannot control how another organisation processes data once it is outside our systems; where a third party sends personal data to us — a referral, a support escalation, a transaction record — we handle it under this policy from the point we receive it.

16. Changes to this policy

16.1 Review and version

We review this policy from time to time; the version published on our website is the current one, and the "Last updated" date at the top shows when it changed.

16.2 Material changes, and continued use

If a change materially affects how personal data is collected or used, we will give notice before or when it takes effect, where practicable — on the website, in the account area, or by email to your account address — and where the law requires consent we will ask for it. If you continue to use our services after an update takes effect, the updated policy applies; if you do not accept it, you may stop using the service and close your account, and we will not use a change to reduce protection for data we already hold.

17. How to contact us

17.1 Where to send a privacy question

For any question about this policy, or to exercise a right in Section 12, contact us using the contact details published on our website, or through the contact channel made available on our website; that is the correct route for privacy requests, and it reaches the team responsible for them.

17.2 What to include, and who handles it

Tell us your name, how we should reach you, the organisation or account concerned, the right you want to exercise, and the outcome you are looking for, because we may ask you to verify your identity before disclosing or changing anything. Enquiries are handled by the team responsible for data protection and compliance; we never ask for your password or full payment details.

17.3 If you are not satisfied

If our answer does not resolve the issue, tell us, because we would rather fix it with you than leave it unresolved; you may also complain to the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data, Hong Kong, whose website at pcpd.org.hk explains the process and the information required.
